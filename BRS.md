## 1. Purpose and document authority

This Business Requirements Specification consolidates the business
requirements and frameworks evaluated and approved for Antenyi Digital
Services.

It is the single coherent BRS. The separate stage documents in this
package are implementation plans derived from this specification; they
do not replace or fragment it.

This document records what the business intends the marketplace to do,
who it serves, the rules governing transactions and operations, and the
controls required to operate responsibly.

### 1.1 Status labels

-   **AGREED:** explicitly approved by the project owner.
-   **PROPOSED:** recommended direction not yet approved as a final
    business decision.
-   **UNRESOLVED:** requires further decision, research or validation.
-   **CONDITIONAL:** approved in principle but activation depends on
    readiness, provider capability, legal review or operational
    capacity.

Where a proposal is included for planning, it must not be treated as an
approved business commitment.

## 2. Business overview and vision

Antenyi Digital Services is a planned Nigeria-first, multi-vendor
marketplace connecting individual consumers, households, retailers,
wholesalers, businesses and organisations with individual sellers,
producers, merchants, established businesses and professional service
providers.

The marketplace is intended to support the discovery, promotion, sale,
purchase and fulfilment of physical products, digital products and
professional services.

The platform is not merely a product catalogue. Its intended
capabilities include seller onboarding, listing approval, search and
discovery, shopping carts, multi-seller checkout, payments, order
management, fulfilment, digital delivery, service quotations, customer
protection, refunds, disputes, seller financial records, administration,
reporting and governance.

### 2.1 Positioning --- AGREED

**A trusted Nigerian multi-category marketplace connecting verified
sellers, producers, retailers and businesses with individual and bulk
buyers through convenient, transparent and reliable digital commerce.**

### 2.2 Strategic pillars --- AGREED

1.  Trust: verified sellers and responsible governance.
2.  Product diversity: multiple permitted categories and offering types.
3.  Business opportunities: individual, retail, wholesale, bulk and
    institutional buyers.
4.  Category-specific information: accurate attributes and measurements.
5.  Market accessibility: practical discovery and nationwide marketplace
    capability, subject to operational coverage.
6.  Transaction reliability: transparent prices, verified payments and
    traceable fulfilment.

### 2.3 Product principles --- AGREED

-   Trust before growth.
-   Security and privacy by design.
-   Transparent pricing and explainable financial records.
-   Independent seller-order fulfilment.
-   Evidence-based payment, delivery, refund and payout statuses.
-   Accessible and mobile-friendly experience.
-   Practical simplicity and incremental delivery.
-   International readiness without unsupported availability claims.

## 3. Business objectives

The marketplace shall aim to:

1.  Provide a trusted digital marketplace for approved sellers and
    buyers.
2.  Enable individuals and businesses to sell and purchase through
    appropriate workflows.
3.  Support retail, wholesale, bulk and quotation-based purchasing.
4.  Make product and service discovery useful across different
    categories.
5.  Support multiple sellers in one checkout where fulfilment and
    payment rules permit.
6.  Provide transparent and traceable payment and financial workflows.
7.  Protect buyers and legitimate sellers through fair complaint and
    dispute procedures.
8.  Maintain proportionate seller and category verification.
9.  Provide accountable administration and measurable operational
    oversight.
10. Expand categories and geographic coverage only when commercially,
    legally and operationally ready.

## 4. Marketplace scope and category strategy

### 4.1 Marketplace strategy --- AGREED

Adopt a **hybrid marketplace strategy**:

-   Design the platform to support a broad range of permitted products
    and services.
-   Concentrate initial seller acquisition, marketing, customer
    acquisition and operational support on selected priority categories.
-   Introduce additional categories progressively as demand, resources,
    verification and support capacity develop.
-   Technical capability to support a category does not automatically
    authorise commercial launch.

### 4.2 Approved category direction --- AGREED

The following categories are within the marketplace's intended scope:

-   Fashion and clothing.
-   Footwear.
-   Bags and accessories.
-   Local agricultural products, including yam, cassava, garri, rice and
    beans.
-   Timber and other suitable wood products.
-   Digital products.
-   Professional services.
-   Additional permitted categories after assessment.

Timber and wood products are a distinct wood/forestry trading category.
They must not be grouped as food agricultural produce.

The approval of these categories does not mean all must launch in
Release 1.

### 4.3 Category sequencing

Previously recommended commercial sequencing remains a planning
recommendation, not an unconditional launch decision:

-   Initial commercial focus: fashion, bags, footwear and accessories.
-   Additional approved scope: selected agricultural produce and
    timber/wood products, subject to stronger category-specific
    readiness.
-   Digital products and professional services: intended marketplace
    offering types, activated when secure delivery, rights, service and
    dispute workflows are ready.
-   Home/lifestyle: limited, selected expansion.
-   Electronics: deferred until seller verification, authenticity,
    warranty, returns and dispute controls are mature.

Exact initial categories, geographic launch locations and launch dates
remain subject to commercial and operational review.

## 5. Geographic and market strategy

### 5.1 Nigeria-first --- AGREED

The initial market direction is Nigeria.

The platform should be designed to support nationwide seller
registration, while seller recruitment, buyer acquisition and
operational support may initially concentrate in selected locations
where reliable service can be maintained.

Sellers may declare their actual delivery coverage. The marketplace must
not imply nationwide delivery where a seller or logistics arrangement
cannot provide it.

### 5.2 International expansion --- CONDITIONAL

International expansion may be considered only after a country-specific
assessment covering:

-   Legal and business eligibility.
-   Payment acceptance and seller settlement.
-   Currency and foreign-exchange handling.
-   Tax and invoicing.
-   Consumer protection and returns.
-   Privacy and cross-border data transfers.
-   Logistics, customs and product restrictions.
-   Seller verification.
-   Customer support and operational capacity.

No international country is approved for launch by this BRS.

## 6. Users and business participants

### 6.1 Buyers --- AGREED

The marketplace shall serve:

-   Individual consumers.
-   Households.
-   Retailers and resellers.
-   Small and medium-sized businesses.
-   Businesses and organisations, including hotels, restaurants,
    schools, offices, food businesses and woodworking businesses.
-   Bulk and wholesale buyers.
-   Institutional and larger procurement buyers as capability develops.

The platform shall support retail and wholesale transactions.
Seller-defined minimum order quantities and quantity-based pricing may
be used where appropriate. Large or specialised orders may use quotation
requests.

Exact institutional procurement workflows remain unresolved.

### 6.2 Sellers --- AGREED

The marketplace shall accept, subject to approval and verification:

-   Individual sellers.
-   Small businesses.
-   Established businesses.
-   Fashion businesses, designers and retailers.
-   Footwear and bag makers and retailers.
-   Farmers and agricultural producers.
-   Agricultural processors and merchants.
-   Timber farmers, lawful harvesters, processors, merchants and
    finished wood-product sellers.
-   Digital product creators and authorised distributors.
-   Professional service providers.

Seller verification shall be proportionate to seller type, product
category, risk and applicable law. Company registration must not be
imposed on every individual seller where it is not legally or
operationally required.

### 6.3 Public visitors --- AGREED

Visitors may browse public marketplace information without an account.
They must not access private accounts, orders, protected files, seller
dashboards or administration functions.

## 7. Buyer accounts and shopping experience

### 7.1 Browsing and registration --- AGREED

-   Public browsing is available without registration.
-   Buyer registration or sign-in is required before checkout.
-   Support individual and business buyer accounts.
-   Business accounts may have additional purchasing and
    record-management features.
-   Business status does not automatically grant credit or deferred
    payment.

### 7.2 Customer account information

Collect only information reasonably required for account operation,
order fulfilment, legal obligations and security. Support secure account
recovery, profile updates, address management, communication preferences
and account closure subject to lawful record-retention obligations.

### 7.3 Discovery --- AGREED

Provide category browsing, keyword search, seller/shop discovery,
relevant filters, sorting, product/service details and verified-purchase
reviews.

Filters must be category-aware. Examples include size and colour for
clothing; variety, origin and measurement unit for produce; species and
dimensions for timber; licence and format for digital products; and
scope or delivery time for services.

Paid placements, when introduced, must be clearly labelled and must not
deceptively distort ordinary search results.

### 7.4 Shopping cart and multi-seller orders --- AGREED

Support eligible multi-seller carts. A successful checkout creates:

-   One parent customer order.
-   Separate seller orders for each participating seller.

Each seller order must be independently managed for fulfilment,
cancellation, refund, dispute and seller allocation. Mixed physical,
digital and service combinations may be restricted where necessary to
preserve correct payment and fulfilment behaviour.

### 7.5 Transparent checkout --- AGREED

Before order confirmation, show the buyer:

-   Items and quantities.
-   Seller identity.
-   Product/service prices.
-   Separate delivery charges for each seller shipment.
-   Applicable fees, discounts and taxes where established.
-   Delivery or collection method.
-   Complete payable total.
-   Relevant terms and conditions.
-   Payment method and next steps.

No hidden charge may be introduced after the buyer confirms the order.
Where a quotation is required, the buyer must receive and accept it
before the relevant purchase is confirmed.

### 7.6 Customer dashboard --- AGREED

Provide a customer area for orders, payments, delivery, digital
purchases, service projects, complaints, refunds, messages,
notifications and account settings.

## 8. Seller onboarding, shops and listings

### 8.1 Seller onboarding --- AGREED

Seller applications require administrator approval before selling.

Recommended workflow:

1.  Account creation.
2.  Seller type and category selection.
3.  Collection of proportionate identity, business and category
    information.
4.  Verification review.
5.  Approval, rejection or request for additional information.
6.  Shop setup.
7.  First listing submission and administrator approval.
8.  Subsequent listing privileges according to seller trust status and
    category risk.

### 8.2 Listing approval --- AGREED

New sellers' first listings require administrator approval.
Trusted-seller privileges may permit later listings to be published
without individual manual review, but must be earned, category-limited,
monitored, configurable and revocable.

Listing states should include draft, submitted, under review, more
information required, approved, rejected, suspended, removed and
archived.

### 8.3 Seller dashboard --- AGREED

Provide appropriate tools for shop profile, listings, inventory, orders,
fulfilment, enquiries, quotations, reviews, performance, earnings,
payouts, support and verification status.

Seller data must be isolated. A seller must not access another seller's
private orders, buyers, documents or financial records.

## 9. Category-specific requirements

### 9.1 Agricultural produce and processed products --- AGREED

The marketplace shall distinguish fresh produce from processed
agricultural products.

Listings should use accurate descriptions, practical standardised
measurements and clear units. Traditional units such as bags, baskets or
bunches may be used when relevant, with weight or quantity disclosure
where appropriate.

Sellers must make truthful claims about grade, condition, origin,
packaging, storage, handling and certification. Available farm,
processor, supplier or batch traceability information should be recorded
proportionately.

Higher-risk food-safety concerns must be escalated promptly. Credibly
affected listings or batches may be temporarily restricted while
investigated. Category-specific complaint reporting periods and evidence
requirements must be developed.

### 9.2 Timber and wood products --- AGREED

Accept legitimate timber farmers, lawful harvesters, processors,
merchants and finished wood-product sellers.

Apply proportionate seller verification and reasonable sourcing/purchase
evidence. Verify permits, licences, legal authorisations and transport
documents where required. Do not demand a harvesting permit from a
reseller where the law does not require one.

Listings must accurately disclose species, dimensions, quantities,
condition, treatment and quality claims. Prohibit knowingly unlawful
sourcing and restricted/protected products unless required
authorisations are established. Credible illegal-sourcing reports must
be escalated and affected listings or transactions may be restricted
during investigation.

Commercial activation is conditional on suitable verification, legal
guidance and transportation arrangements. International/export trading
requires separate assessment.

### 9.3 Digital products --- AGREED

Sellers must hold distribution and intellectual-property rights.
Listings must explain product content, format, compatibility and
licence/usage terms. Delivery must be secure and enabled only after
verified payment. Copyright complaints, takedown procedures, protected
access and relevant refund rules must be established.

### 9.4 Professional services --- AGREED

Support fixed-price and quotation-based services.

An accepted quotation must record scope, deliverables, price, timeline,
revision terms and payment conditions. Scope changes require recorded
mutual agreement. Ownership/licensing for commissioned work must be
agreed in advance. Qualifications must be checked where legally
required. Milestone arrangements are conditional on provider and legal
capability; the platform must not claim escrow or fund holding without
support.

## 10. Pricing, revenue and commercial model

### 10.1 Revenue direction --- AGREED

The preferred initial model is seller-funded transaction commission.
Buyer registration, seller registration and standard listings are
initially free. Compulsory monthly subscriptions are not recommended for
launch.

Additional possible future revenue includes clearly disclosed promoted
listings, advertising, optional subscriptions, procurement services,
logistics coordination and seller services.

### 10.2 Commission --- UNRESOLVED DETAILS

No specific percentage or fixed amount is approved. A previously
discussed 5% example was illustrative only.

Before implementation, determine: - Exact commission formula and rate. -
Whether rates vary by category. - Rounding and tax treatment. - Payment
processing fee allocation. - Treatment of discounts. -
Refund/cancellation commission reversal. - Seller statement and invoice
presentation. - Financial reporting and accounting treatment.

The older concept of automatically adding a platform contribution to a
seller's desired net price is not a final approved pricing rule. The
current agreed direction is seller-funded commission, subject to a
transparent and financially viable formula.

### 10.3 Fee transparency --- AGREED

Buyers must see the full amount payable before confirmation. Sellers
must understand the commission and other disclosed deductions and their
expected proceeds.

## 11. Payments and financial management

### 11.1 Payment methods --- AGREED / CONDITIONAL

-   Cards and bank transfers are intended initial methods, subject to
    provider capability and onboarding.
-   USSD may be included if practical; otherwise it is a later option.
-   Cash on delivery is not a standard launch option.
-   Full payment is supported.
-   Deposit-and-balance arrangements may be supported for approved
    transactions with documented terms.
-   No credit facilities initially.
-   Marketplace orders should use approved platform payment channels;
    sellers should not be encouraged to divert buyers to personal
    accounts.

The payment provider is not selected.

### 11.2 Payment verification --- AGREED

-   Verify payments through authenticated provider notifications and
    reconciliation.
-   Validate transaction reference, amount, currency and order
    association.
-   Do not accept screenshots or browser redirects alone as proof.
-   Use unique references and idempotent processing.
-   Protect against forged, replayed or duplicate webhook events.
-   Track pending, failed, successful, reversed and refunded states.
-   Do not release goods, digital access or seller earnings based only
    on unverified claims.

### 11.3 Financial ledger --- AGREED

Maintain auditable records for payments, sales, commissions, fees,
refunds, reversals, earnings, payouts, failures and obligations.
Financial history must not be silently overwritten. Corrections should
use authorised, traceable adjustment or reversal entries.

### 11.4 Seller payouts --- AGREED / CONDITIONAL

-   Seller proceeds reflect gross sales less agreed commission and other
    disclosed applicable deductions.
-   Payout eligibility follows verified fulfilment/completion and
    applicable dispute conditions.
-   Standard weekly payout schedule is preferred, subject to provider
    capability, legal requirements and final operational design.
-   Sellers should be able to see sales, deductions, eligible earnings
    and payout history.
-   Failed payouts must be recorded, investigated and safely retried.
-   Do not assume the platform can hold funds or offer escrow.

## 12. Orders, cancellations and fulfilment

### 12.1 Order structure --- AGREED

One parent order with independent seller orders. Parent and seller-order
states must not imply that every seller has completed fulfilment when
only one has.

### 12.2 Cancellation --- AGREED

-   Buyer may cancel before seller acceptance without penalty.
-   After acceptance, cancellation follows order and fulfilment
    conditions.
-   Customised and made-to-order goods may have special terms disclosed
    before purchase.
-   After dispatch, ordinary cancellation becomes a return/refund
    matter.
-   Sellers must give valid reasons for cancelling accepted orders.
-   Sellers must not retain payment for unfulfilled orders improperly.
-   External circumstances receive fair assessment.
-   Repeated seller cancellations may affect performance.
-   Decisions and financial adjustments are recorded.
-   Applicable consumer rights take precedence.

### 12.3 Fulfilment --- AGREED / CONDITIONAL

Support: 1. Seller-managed delivery. 2. Platform-supported delivery
through approved logistics partners where available. 3. Buyer
collection.

Delivery charges must be calculated and disclosed separately for each
seller shipment. Sellers declare actual coverage. Bulk orders may
require transport quotations. Perishable agricultural products and bulky
timber require category-appropriate handling. Do not claim a logistics
integration or coverage until implemented and tested.

## 13. Returns, refunds, complaints and disputes

### 13.1 Customer protection --- AGREED

Buyers may report: - Damaged, defective, incorrect or misrepresented
products. - Missing items or quantity discrepancies. - Undelivered or
incomplete orders. - Service non-delivery or material failure. -
Relevant digital-product access or licensing problems.

Each complaint receives a unique reference. Sellers have an appropriate
opportunity to respond. Evidence is considered. Full refunds, partial
refunds and replacements are supported where appropriate. Buyers should
not bear the cost of correcting seller-caused mistakes. Fraudulent
claims and dishonest seller practices are investigated. Decisions and
financial adjustments are recorded.

### 13.2 Category-aware rules --- AGREED

Reporting periods, evidence and remedies must vary appropriately by
category. Fresh produce may require prompt reporting; timber may require
inspection and transport evidence; digital access and service milestones
require their own rules. Category rules must not remove mandatory
consumer rights.

### 13.3 Dispute escalation --- AGREED

Three levels: 1. Buyer and seller attempt resolution through the
platform. 2. Platform support investigates unresolved matters. 3.
Authorised administrator reviews serious, repeated or financially
significant disputes and makes the platform's decision, subject to
applicable legal rights.

Maintain evidence, communications, decisions, responsible officers and
financial consequences. The process must be impartial and support
appropriate review/appeal arrangements.

## 14. Reviews, communication and notifications

### 14.1 Reviews --- AGREED DIRECTION

Verified-purchase reviews are intended. Product reviews and seller
performance assessments should be distinct. Moderation, eligibility,
editing, seller responses and abuse handling require detailed rules.

### 14.2 Communication --- AGREED DIRECTION

Provide website enquiry forms, order-related communication and
email/in-platform notifications. WhatsApp, SMS and real-time chat are
future possibilities, not assumed launch integrations.

Protect contact details and restrict access to transaction
communications according to role and purpose.

### 14.3 Notifications --- AGREED DIRECTION

Essential notifications include account verification/security, order and
payment events, fulfilment, complaints, refunds, quotations and relevant
service updates. Marketing messages must be distinguishable from
essential messages, subject to appropriate consent and unsubscribe
controls.

## 15. Administration, governance and internal operations

### 15.1 Administrative roles --- AGREED

Functional roles include: - Platform Owner / Executive Administrator. -
Operations Manager. - Seller Verification Officer. - Catalogue and
Listing Moderator. - Finance Officer. - Customer Support Officer. -
Seller Support Officer. - Dispute Resolution Officer. - Risk and
Compliance Officer. - Technical Administrator. - Internal Auditor /
Reviewer.

These are functions, not a requirement to hire eleven separate people at
launch. Multiple duties may be assigned to a small team, but sensitive
duties must be separated wherever practical.

### 15.2 Staff identity and permissions --- AGREED

Each staff member has an individual account; shared administrator
accounts are prohibited. Use least-privilege, role-based permissions,
defined approval authority, temporary access where needed and prompt
revocation when access is no longer required.

### 15.3 Seller and listing governance --- AGREED

Seller verification and listing moderation follow documented workflows.
Higher-risk categories receive additional checks. Exceptional approvals
require reasons, evidence and authorised review. Trusted-seller
privileges are earned, category-limited, monitored and revocable.

### 15.4 Financial controls --- AGREED

Separate financial preparation, approval and execution wherever
practical. Use approval limits and two-person approval for sensitive or
exceptional operations. Staff must not directly edit seller balances.
Financial corrections use auditable adjustment entries.

### 15.5 Disputes and conflicts --- AGREED

Use tiered authority. Staff with personal or financial conflicts must
declare them and be removed from the affected decision. Serious cases
receive independent review.

### 15.6 Support operations --- AGREED DIRECTION

Use case references, categories, assignment, priority, status,
communications and resolution records. Do not promise 24-hour or
immediate support until staffing supports it.

### 15.7 Fraud and compliance --- AGREED DIRECTION

Monitor suspicious seller identity, payment, refund, review, account,
payout and listing activity. Alerts are not proof. Serious enforcement
receives appropriate human review. Temporary protective restrictions may
be applied where credible risk requires them and must be documented and
reviewed.

### 15.8 Audit trails --- AGREED

Record important administrative, financial, security and policy actions
with actor, time, affected record, action, reason and relevant
before/after values or approval references. Ordinary administrators must
not be able to delete or rewrite audit history.

### 15.9 Internal security --- AGREED

Use stronger security for privileged accounts, multi-factor
authentication where appropriate, restricted sensitive-data access,
secure sessions, permission reviews, conflict management and controlled
emergency access with retrospective review.

### 15.10 Management reporting --- AGREED DIRECTION

Provide role-appropriate business, financial and operational reports.
Distinguish gross transaction value from platform revenue. Avoid
unnecessary personal-data exposure.

### 15.11 Policy governance --- AGREED DIRECTION

Maintain version-controlled public policies and internal operating
procedures, with effective dates, approval authority, review dates and
change history.

### 15.12 Additional governance controls --- AGREED

-   Formal approval matrix.
-   Two-person approval for sensitive operations.
-   Conflict-of-interest management.
-   Staff performance and accountability.
-   Periodic governance and permission reviews.

The owner retains appropriate executive authority, but executive actions
affecting financial records, security, sensitive data and audit history
must remain traceable and protected.

## 16. Technical and quality requirements

### 16.1 Approved technical direction --- AGREED

-   Python backend.
-   Django framework.
-   Modular monolithic architecture.
-   PostgreSQL production database.
-   Django ORM.
-   Django templates with HTML, CSS, JavaScript and Bootstrap initially.
-   Provider-independent integration boundaries.
-   Django authentication with role-based and object-level
    authorisation.
-   Separate development, testing/staging and production environments.
-   Automated backups and tested recovery.
-   Application and infrastructure monitoring.
-   Automated and manual testing.
-   Extensible design for future categories, markets and mobile
    applications.

SQLite may be used for suitable local experimentation, but PostgreSQL is
the intended production database.

### 16.2 Architecture principles --- AGREED

-   Avoid premature microservices.
-   Keep domain modules cohesive with explicit responsibilities.
-   Use database constraints and transactions for critical operations.
-   Preserve historical order, price, policy and financial snapshots.
-   Make payment and other retryable processing idempotent.
-   Use explicit state transitions.
-   Enforce ownership and permissions on the server.
-   Keep external providers behind integration interfaces.
-   Do not create speculative infrastructure without a real need.

### 16.3 Security --- AGREED DIRECTION

Security is designed into every feature. Controls include secure
authentication, object-level authorisation, CSRF/XSS/injection
protections, rate limiting, safe uploads, protected downloads, secret
management, HTTPS/TLS, webhook validation, audit logs, dependency
maintenance and backup protection.

OWASP ASVS Level 2 is a proposed security verification baseline; this is
not a claim of certification or completed compliance.

### 16.4 Accessibility and responsive design --- AGREED DIRECTION

Mobile-first responsive design. The marketplace should be usable on
phones without horizontal page scrolling. WCAG 2.2 AA is the proposed
accessibility target, subject to implementation and testing.

### 16.5 Performance and reliability --- AGREED DIRECTION

Optimise database queries, images, pagination and caching where
appropriate. Introduce background processing, additional application
instances or other scaling mechanisms when measured demand justifies
them. Define production service, performance and recovery targets before
launch.

## 17. Legal, regulatory and policy readiness

**UNRESOLVED --- mandatory evaluation required.**

The legal/regulatory framework has not yet been fully evaluated in the
requirements process. Before real transactions or public launch, obtain
appropriate Nigerian legal, tax, privacy, consumer-protection,
payment-provider and category-specific reviews.

Areas requiring assessment include: - Business registration and
marketplace operating structure. - Consumer protection and e-commerce
obligations. - Nigeria Data Protection Act and applicable NDPC
requirements. - Payment-provider and settlement model requirements. -
Tax, invoicing and accounting obligations. - Food and processed
agricultural product requirements. - Timber sourcing, forestry,
protected species and transport requirements. - Intellectual property
and digital-content distribution. - Professional licensing where
applicable. - Advertising, promotions and competition considerations. -
Employment and staff data obligations. - International obligations
before any foreign market is activated.

This BRS is not legal advice and does not establish compliance.

## 18. Non-functional requirements

The platform should provide: - Confidentiality and appropriate access
isolation. - Integrity of order, payment and financial records. -
Availability appropriate to the agreed operational capacity. -
Recoverability through tested backups. - Maintainability through modular
code and documentation. - Observability through logs, metrics and
alerts. - Usability and accessibility. - Scalability based on measured
demand. - Interoperability through controlled integration interfaces. -
Auditability of sensitive operations.

Exact numerical targets remain unresolved until hosting, operational
capacity and risk are assessed.

## 19. Reporting and success measures

Management should be able to review: - Active buyers and sellers. -
Seller application and listing approval queues. - Sales and completed
orders. - Gross transaction value and platform revenue separately. -
Commission, fees, refunds and payouts. - Fulfilment and cancellation
performance. - Complaint and dispute volumes and resolution. - Fraud and
compliance incidents. - Support response and resolution. - Category
performance and repeat purchasing.

Targets should be set after a pilot and based on observed data rather
than invented in advance.

## 21. Release 1 boundary

Release 1 should deliver a coherent, safe marketplace, not every future
feature.

Intended capabilities include: - Public browsing. - Buyer
registration/sign-in before checkout. - Seller applications and
approval. - Individual and business seller profiles. - First-listing
approval and configurable trusted-seller privileges. - Approved
physical, digital and service listings. - Search and discovery. -
Eligible multi-seller cart and parent/seller order structure. - Card and
bank transfer through a selected, approved provider. - Verified payment
handling. - Order management. - Supported physical fulfilment methods. -
Secure digital delivery. - Fixed-price and quotation-based services. -
Refund and dispute foundations. - Verified-purchase reviews. -
Website/email notifications and basic enquiries. - Administrative
oversight and audit records.

The exact category mix and functionality activated in the pilot must be
constrained by legal, provider, verification, fulfilment and support
readiness. Do not simulate an unavailable capability.

## 22. Acceptance and definition of done

A feature is not complete merely because its page renders.

Each feature or milestone must have: - A clear requirement and
acceptance criteria. - Defined user roles and permission boundaries. -
Normal, failure and abuse scenarios. - Server-side validation and
data-integrity controls. - Security and privacy review. - Passing
automated tests. - Relevant manual checks. - Accurate user-facing
statuses and messages. - Updated documentation. - Recorded limitations
and known risks.

A production launch additionally requires legal, provider, security,
privacy, accessibility, financial reconciliation, support, backup,
